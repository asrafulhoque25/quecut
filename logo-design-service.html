Sound familiar?

The best logo projects tend to start at a turning point, not on a random afternoon. These are the four moments that most often bring brands to us — find the one that fits, and you'll know a new logo is your right next move.

You're launching something new

A business, product, or venture about to meet the world for the first time. Your logo is the very first impression you'll make — and first impressions compound across every touchpoint that follows.

You've outgrown a DIY logo

You bootstrapped an identity with a free maker, Canva, or an AI generator to get moving fast. It did its job early on — but now it quietly undersells how far you've actually come.

Your brand needs a refresh

An established company whose current mark has started to feel dated, cluttered, or inconsistent from one place to the next as the business has grown and evolved.

You need a mark you truly own

You've realised your "logo" is really a stock graphic or a shared template — a genuine problem the moment you try to trademark it, scale it, or make it properly yours.

Geometry, not guesswork

Every mark we create is constructed on a geometric grid with deliberate proportion, spacing, and optical balance. That discipline is what separates a logo that merely looks fine from one that feels genuinely resolved. It's also what keeps your logo sharp at sixteen pixels in a browser tab and commanding at three metres on a trade-show banner. We obsess over the details most people never consciously notice — the curve of a corner, the weight of a line — because those details are exactly what make a mark feel trustworthy and timeless.

A mark they finally own

When Cadence came to us, they were launching on a marketplace logo that three of their competitors were also using — hardly the distinct first impression a new brand needs. We designed an original wordmark with a custom ligature and a scalable submark, giving them a mark that was unmistakably theirs and fully trademark-ready. The result was cleaner, more memorable, and consistent everywhere from their app icon to their trade-show banner, and it more than doubled brand recall in follow-up customer surveys.
